# App store listing copy

**The line:** *Ask for it. Sellers come to you.*

Chosen 2 September 2026. It appears verbatim in four places and must stay
identical in all of them: the website `<title>` and `<h1>`, the Open Graph
tags, the app's home screen banner, and the store listings below.

The reason to be strict about this is not tidiness. A person meets a
positioning line three or four times before it means anything — store
listing, then website, then the app itself. If each surface paraphrases it,
those exposures don't stack, and it reads as three products with a family
resemblance rather than one product said three times. Paraphrasing is the
single most common way a good line is wasted. Do not "freshen" one surface
without changing all of them.

## Google Play

### App name — max 30 characters

```
ImbizoHub
```
*(9 chars)*

Leaving the name clean. The temptation is to stuff it — "ImbizoHub: Zimbabwe
Marketplace" (32) is over the limit anyway, and Play ranks on the full
listing text, not just the title.

### Short description — max 80 characters

This is the line's most important placement. It is what shows in search
results, under the icon, before anyone taps through.

```
Ask for it. Sellers come to you. Post what you need and get prices back.
```
*(71 chars)*

## Apple App Store

### App name — max 30 characters

```
ImbizoHub
```

### Subtitle — max 30 characters

The full line is **32 characters**, two over Apple's limit. Do not silently
truncate it into something that reads oddly — shorten it deliberately:

```
Ask. Sellers come to you.
```
*(25 chars)*

The full sentence still opens the description, so the complete line is the
first thing a reader sees on the page itself.

### Promotional text — max 170 characters

```
Ask for it. Sellers come to you. Post what you need — a fridge, a van, a builder — and get prices back. Free to ask, free to chat, free to do a deal.
```
*(147 chars)*

### Description

Use the Google Play full description above unchanged.


## Transport keywords

The transport front door lives at **imbizohub.com/transport**. Its search
intent is completely separate from the marketplace's — somebody typing "van
hire Harare" is not browsing for a second-hand fridge — and as of September
2026 nobody in the Zimbabwean market is competing for these terms in an app
listing at all.

Both stores rank on the full description, so the transport paragraph in the
listing above is what earns this traffic. Keep these phrases in it verbatim:

```
van hire Harare · van hire Bulawayo · truck hire Zimbabwe
moving and removals Zimbabwe · transport Harare to Bulawayo
hire a van Zimbabwe · goods transport Zimbabwe · courier Zimbabwe
airport transfer Harare · group transport Zimbabwe
```

**Honesty constraint, and it matters more than the ranking.** Every operator
registered today drives a van (`profiles.vehicle_capacity = 8` across the
board). So "truck hire Zimbabwe" is worth including only for the searches it
catches — the listing text itself must keep saying *vans, up to a van load*.
If somebody arrives from a truck search and posts a house move, that request
sits unquoted, which is exactly what happened to the Kwekwe → Plumtree job on
1 September. An unanswered request costs more than a missed install.

Revisit this whole section the day an operator with a truck registers.

## Before you can submit

Both stores check these, and both will reject on them:

- **Privacy policy URL must resolve and be complete.** Ours still contains
  `[TO CONFIRM]` placeholders. This is the most likely cause of a rejection
  and it is entirely within our control to fix today.
- **Account deletion** must be reachable — we have `account-delete.tsx`, so
  note its in-app path on the data-safety form.
- **Data safety / privacy nutrition labels** must declare location, photos,
  messages and contact details.
- Screenshots: at least 2 for Play (recommend 5–8), and a 1024×1024 icon.
